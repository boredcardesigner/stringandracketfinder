Arm-friendly tennis strings

108 of the 354 beds here rate 8 or better on arm friendliness; 9 rate 3 or below. That axis is the one most string charts leave out, and it is the reason this list exists.

Open the string bench →

This is equipment guidance, not medical advice. Persistent elbow, wrist or shoulder pain is worth showing to a physiotherapist or doctor — string choice can reduce load, but it cannot treat an injury, and technique and racket stiffness usually matter more than either.

Where the shock comes from

Three things stack: a stiff frame, a stiff string, and high tension. Any one is manageable; all three together is the setup behind most club-level arm trouble. Stiff polyester became the default because it dominates professional tennis, but professionals restring constantly, swing differently, and have staff. A poly that is four sessions old has lost much of its elasticity and is harsher than the day it went in.

What actually helps

Softer material first — multifilament or natural gut, or a gut hybrid if you want to keep some bite. Lower tension second; a few pounds down does more for comfort than most string swaps. Fresh strings third, because dead poly is a hard bed regardless of what it was when new. And look at the frame: a high RA value with a stiff string is the combination to avoid, which is why the finder scores the pairing rather than the string on its own.

Notes

BABOLAT RPM Blast 1.25

The reference spin polyester. An octagonal profile bites the ball and the stiff, low-powered response keeps everything short and controlled, so you can swing fully without sailing. Expect heavy shape and reliable depth control; expect also a firm feel and a fairly quick drop in tension. Not a comfort string — pair it with gut in a hybrid if your arm objects.

TOROLINE O-Toro

A rounder, softer counterpart to the Wasabi range — more comfort and more dwell time, less outright spin bite. A good option for a player who wants Toroline's control character in a bed that does not beat up the arm, or as crosses under a sharper main string.

WILSON Revolve Spin

A shaped spin poly built for grab, sitting at a friendlier price than the tour-level options. Expect solid bite and reasonable control with a firm feel. A practical everyday spin string rather than a precision instrument — good value for a player who restrings often.

VÖLKL V-Square 1.20

A shaped spin polyester with genuinely aggressive bite — the square-ish profile grabs the ball hard. Expect big spin potential and firm, controlled depth. Like most sharply shaped strings, the edges round off and the bite fades before the string actually breaks, so its best window is short.
