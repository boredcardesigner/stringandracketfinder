Model notes

Pure Aero 2016

The frame that defined the modern spin category. Expect enormous topspin, a high launch and easy, heavy depth almost regardless of how cleanly you strike it. The trade-offs are equally clear: it is stiff, it can feel harsh on mishits, and flat driving is less precise than the spin numbers suggest. String it soft. A weapon for a swinging, high-margin baseliner.

Pure Aero 100

The current Aero, and still the clearest statement of the spin-first idea. Expect enormous topspin, a high launch and heavy depth that arrives with very little effort from you. Later generations softened the harshness of the 2016-era frames without losing the character. The limit is the same as always: flat, low driving is less precise than the spin numbers suggest, and control depends on you actually swinging.

Pure Aero 98

The Aero's control-biased sibling — a smaller head and tighter bed that pulls the launch angle down and the precision up while keeping most of the spin identity. It gives up the Aero 100's free depth and its enormous margin, and in exchange you can actually flatten a ball out. The best Aero for a player who liked the spin but not the lack of a ceiling.

Choosing between them

Frames in one family share a construction story but not a playing character — head size, pattern and swingweight move the numbers above far more than the marketing does.
