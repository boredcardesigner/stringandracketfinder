Tennis string hybrids

A hybrid puts one string in the mains and a different one in the crosses. 148 of the 350 beds here are hybrids, rated as their own thing — because a hybrid does not play like the average of its two strings.

Open the string bench →

Why hybrid at all

Almost every hybrid exists to resolve one trade: polyester gives bite, control and durability but transmits shock and goes dead; multifilament and natural gut give comfort and feel but move, fray and lose tension. Putting the poly in the mains and the softer string in the crosses keeps most of the bite — the mains do the snapback — while the crosses take some of the harshness out.

Which string goes where

The mains run the length of the racket and do most of the work: they stretch further, move more, and wear out first. That is why the durable, slick string almost always goes there. The crosses mainly govern how the bed feels and how quickly it settles. Reverse the order — gut mains, poly crosses — and you get the classic professional setup: enormous feel and power, at a price and a lifespan most club players will not accept.

Tension

A hybrid is usually strung with the crosses a little lower than the mains when the crosses are the softer string, so the softer material is not stretched into stiffness. Ask your stringer rather than guessing; the right offset depends on both materials.

Notes

BABOLAT RPM Blast 1.25

The reference spin polyester. An octagonal profile bites the ball and the stiff, low-powered response keeps everything short and controlled, so you can swing fully without sailing. Expect heavy shape and reliable depth control; expect also a firm feel and a fairly quick drop in tension. Not a comfort string — pair it with gut in a hybrid if your arm objects.

TOROLINE Wasabi

A shaped co-poly with a reputation for sharp spin bite at a friendlier price than the tour brands. Expect strong grab on the ball and a firm, lively response. Its playing window is fairly short — the bite fades before the string breaks — so it rewards players who restring on a schedule rather than on failure.

TOROLINE O-Toro

A rounder, softer counterpart to the Wasabi range — more comfort and more dwell time, less outright spin bite. A good option for a player who wants Toroline's control character in a bed that does not beat up the arm, or as crosses under a sharper main string.

SOLINCO Mach 10

A firm, low-powered control polyester aimed at players who swing hard and want the stringbed to keep everything in front of them. Expect a very direct, muted response and excellent depth control on full-blooded strokes. It gives back little on a short swing — this is a string that assumes you are generating your own pace.

SOLINCO Hyper-G

One of the most popular spin polys outside the big brands. Sharply shaped, low powered and very controlled, with a distinctly firm, muscular feel. Expect big spin and confident depth control on full swings. It is not soft — players who need comfort usually meet it halfway in a hybrid.
