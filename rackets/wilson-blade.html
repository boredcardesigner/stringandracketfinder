WILSON BLADE tennis rackets compared

FORTYFIVE° — carbon laid at 45° so the hoop “flexes with you” — plus DirectConnect, a carbon handle fused straight to the hoop for torsional calm. Both real, both subtle; the v8 colour-shift paint moved more units than any layup ever has. The Blade's true tech is its pattern discipline: 18×20 for the surgeons, 16×19 for everyone else.

Model notes

Blade 98 v8

Wilson's all-court control frame, and the more comfortable of the modern 98s. The flexible layup gives noticeable dwell time and a plush, connected impact — you feel where the ball is on the bed. Expect very good precision and touch with moderate power, meaning you supply pace but rarely fight the racquet to place it. Strong on slice and at the net, honest from the baseline.

Blade 98 18x20

The tighter-patterned Blade: lower launch, tighter dispersion, and a more deliberate ball than the 16x19. Best suited to a flat, driving player whose errors go long. The dense bed cuts spin access noticeably, so if you rely on shape to bring the ball down, the 16x19 is the more natural choice. Feel remains plush for a control frame.

Choosing between them

Frames in one family share a construction story but not a playing character — head size, pattern and swingweight move the numbers above far more than the marketing does.
